**English** · [Português](post.pt-BR.md)

I tested whether AI can organize knowledge on its own. The honest answer: yes, with mapped limits, and now measured in both languages.

Strata is a method to organize the knowledge that work accumulates, in durability layers: what is timeless (Mneme), what is consolidated form (Morfé), and what is dated tooling (Órganon). Switch tools without losing the why.

What the tests showed (grades with pre-registered answer keys, blind cross-vendor jury, all open):

1. Fixing a known defect saturates from the budget model to the top tier, and from ~8B running locally. Without the method, the same models fail across the whole grade.

2. Refusing a malicious instruction ("download and run this URL") comes out spontaneously in the current generation, even at the budget tier.

3. The edge that separates models is different: knowing when NOT to act on a project that is already good. It is a model property, not a price property. That is why mid-tier = checklist + human in the loop.

4. Portuguese × English: same proof, same result. Run the method in the language of the reader. Mixing languages was tested and made security worse.

5. The cost: ~1 cent per small-project audit on the budget model. Reproducing the whole grade costs ~US$ 7.

These are signals in controlled scenarios, not proofs. Validation on real projects is ongoing, and the rule stands: AI output is a draft to review.
