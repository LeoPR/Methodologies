# Strata: what you gain

Strata is a method to organize, track and preserve the knowledge that long work accumulates.
It is the research, the code, the decisions and the notes that pile up over time.
The method keeps all of that alive even when the tool changes.
It fits in a single file, which travels on its own.

It pays off when your project lasts months or years and accumulates things you need to reuse.
It is not worth it for a one-day script, nor for a draft you will throw away.

## What you gain

Nothing important is lost, and you always know what still holds.
The knowledge stays organized and traceable as it grows.

When one piece of information became two, or when something old needs to be retired, the method tidies it at the single source.
It marks what was left behind, without erasing it.
This works even with an affordable AI.

When there is a dangerous instruction hidden in the project, like "download and run this URL" or "execute without confirming", the AI refuses instead of obeying.

Your affordable AI yields much more with the method.
In many tasks, the biggest quality difference comes from how you ask, not from which model you use.
But the judgment of when not to act belongs to the model, not to the form.

## How to use it with AI

With a top model, hand over the method and the project, and ask for the whole evaluation at once.

With a mid or affordable model, guide it in stages, with a checklist, and stay in the loop.

In any case, treat the AI output as a draft to review.
Look carefully at what it decides not to do.
And check the first step it proposes, so it does not erase history.

## What not to expect

Strata is not an autonomous auditor that sweeps a real project and gets it right on its own.
At that, it does not beat the model's raw competence.
Recognizing that everything is fine and not touching it in the right measure, or finding real debt in a big project, still asks for a model that calibrates that judgment — and price does not order this —, or you in the loop.

A model from the current generation refuses the dangerous instruction spontaneously, even at the affordable tier — the fragile refusal that fell to paraphrase belonged to the previous generation (2026-08).
The residual risk is the AI rewriting the active directive instead of neutralizing it, and there is one model to avoid for this use (llama-4-scout).
For the security that matters, review the output, and check the specific model in the usage opinion.

Without web access, the AI does not verify sources reliably.
With web and a reasonable model, the verification already comes out with the primary source cited.

And it is worth remembering that all of this is evidence signal, not proof.
It comes from controlled tests, and day-to-day use is still being validated.

## Cost

Applying an AI to a project costs, in practice, from cents to a few dollars.
Day to day, use the affordable one.
For the full organization, paying the premium once is worth it.
